## Routers and switches
A **router** forwards traffic between different networks, such as a home LAN and an internet provider network.

A **switch** connects multiple wired devices within a LAN and sends data toward the correct local port.

Some home routers include a built-in switch and Wi-Fi access point.

## Modems, access points, and links
A **modem** or provider gateway connects a subscriber to the provider's access technology when that connection requires one.

A wireless **access point** lets compatible devices join a LAN through Wi-Fi.

**Ethernet** cables commonly link fixed devices to a switch or router. Wi-Fi offers mobility but can be affected by distance, interference and obstacles.

## Choosing connections
- A fixed desktop that needs stable connectivity may benefit from Ethernet.
- A mobile phone typically uses Wi-Fi.
- Internet access depends on a working route through local networking equipment and the service provider.
- Different homes may have one combined gateway instead of separate devices.

## Visual steps
- Desktop | Connects with an Ethernet cable to a switch.
- Switch | Links wired devices on the LAN, then connects to the router or gateway.
- Router or gateway | Forwards traffic to the internet provider (ISP).
- Phone | Connects by Wi-Fi to the access point or router.
- Combined box | One device may do the router, switch and access point jobs together.

## Example
A small classroom has six wired desktop computers and several tablets. The desktops plug into a switch by Ethernet for a stable link. The tablets use Wi-Fi from an access point. Both groups join the same LAN through one router.
