## Network foundations
A **computer network** connects two or more devices using wired or wireless links so they can exchange data or share resources. Phones, laptops, printers and servers can all be networked.

A connection can exist without internet access. Two laptops can exchange files on a local network even if the outside internet is unavailable.

## Local and wide-area networks
A **local area network (LAN)** connects devices over a limited area such as a home, classroom or office.

A **wide area network (WAN)** spans larger distances and can connect multiple LANs.

The **internet** is a global network of interconnected networks. It is not the same thing as a single home Wi-Fi network.

## Everyday examples
- At home, a phone sends a print job to a wireless printer over the LAN.
- At school, computers share a file server.
- A web browser reaches a website across the internet.
- **Wi-Fi** describes a wireless network link. It does not by itself guarantee internet access.

## Visual steps
- Phone and laptop | Devices in the home send data to the home router.
- Home Wi-Fi router | Joins the devices together. This home side is the LAN.
- Printer | Connected on the home side, so it can be used without the internet.
- Connection beyond the router | The link to the provider. This is the WAN and the internet.
- Reading order | Phone and laptop, then router, then the internet. The printer stays on the LAN side.

## Example
A family prints a school form from a phone while the internet is down. The print job works because the phone and printer share the home LAN. Opening an online article still fails until the internet connection returns.
